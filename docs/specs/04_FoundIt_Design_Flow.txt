1. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 The Emotional Context
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every design decision in FoundIt is made in the awareness that the person using
it is in one of two states, and that they are different states requiring
different treatment.

The owner is mildly distressed. They have lost something, they are annoyed with
themselves, and they want the problem to go away. They are not browsing. They
are not exploring. They want to perform one action and get one answer. Anything
that delays that answer — an onboarding carousel, a feature tour, a decorative
animation, a form field they do not understand — is experienced as an obstacle
between them and their earphones.

The finder is mildly virtuous and entirely uninvested. They have picked
something up and are doing a small favour. Their willingness is real but finite,
and it decays with every second of friction. If the form asks for something they
have to think about, a meaningful fraction of them will close the tab and leave
the item where it is.

The design serves both by being fast and legible above all else. The visual
language is quiet. Nothing decorative competes with the content. The single
place the interface is allowed to be expressive is the match card, because that
is the moment the product delivers its value and the moment where a little
confidence-building emphasis is earned.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2 What This Is Not
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

It is not a social product. There are no profiles to browse, no reputation
scores displayed to peers, no comments, no likes. Every one of those would add
surface area, privacy exposure and build time for no contribution to either core
journey.

It is not a marketplace. There is no transaction, no price, no negotiation, and
the visual language deliberately avoids commerce conventions — no "buy" buttons,
no cart, no product-grid aesthetic that would encourage browsing-as-shopping.

It is not a dashboard product. The administrative analytics exist, but they are a
back room. The front of the product is two buttons.

================================================================================
2. DESIGN PRINCIPLES
================================================================================

Six principles, ordered. When two conflict, the earlier wins.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P1 — Speed of comprehension over density of information
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A person scanning a list of forty found items is performing visual pattern
matching, not reading. The card layout therefore leads with the two attributes
that support recognition fastest — a colour swatch and a category icon — and
places text second. A dense table showing eight columns would carry more
information per screen and would be slower to scan, which makes it worse.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P2 — Say what happened, not what the system did
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every message is written from the user's position. "Someone else's claim on this
item was approved a moment ago" rather than "Conflict: row locked". "Choose a
date on or before today" rather than "Constraint violation: ck_lost_date_not_
future". The database's vocabulary never reaches the screen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P3 — Show the reasoning
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The match score is not presented as a verdict. It is presented as an argument
with its premises visible. A user who can see that the score came from category,
brand, colour, location and a one-day gap will trust an 87 and will correctly
distrust a 62. A user shown only the number has no basis for either.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P4 — Make the privacy boundary visible
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The public and private description fields are visually distinct, labelled, and
carry explicit visibility notes. A user who does not understand which of their
words will be shown publicly will either over-share or under-share, and both are
failures. The lock icon and the sentence beneath the field are not decoration;
they are the mechanism by which the verification system gets useful input.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P5 — Design the empty state first
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every list in this product is empty for its first user and empty again for every
user whose report has no matches yet. The empty state is therefore the most
frequently seen state in the early life of the product, and it is designed
before the populated state, not after.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P6 — Never let a destructive action be one tap away
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Dismissing a match, withdrawing a report, approving a claim and disposing of an
item all pass through a confirmation. Dismissing a match additionally offers ten
seconds of undo, because it is the easiest damaging mistake available and the
one with the least visible consequence.

3. VISUAL LANGUAGE — COLOUR
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Approach
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The palette is restrained: one brand hue, one accent, a neutral ramp, and four
semantic colours. Colour carries meaning in exactly three places — status
badges, match bands, and item colour swatches — and nowhere else. Where colour
carries meaning, it is never the only carrier, because roughly one in twelve men
has a colour vision deficiency and because a badge that reads only as "green" is
useless in a screenshot printed in monochrome for a report.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 Palette
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    --brand-500   #1C6FB0   Hover
    --brand-100   #DCEBF7   Selected backgrounds, subtle fills
    --brand-050   #F1F7FC   Page tint

  Accent (used only for the match-confidence emphasis)
    --accent-600  #B4520A
    --accent-100  #FBE9D8

  Neutrals

    --ink-700     #3A444E   Secondary text

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3 Colour Semantics
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Status              Colour       Also indicated by
  ------------------  -----------  ------------------------------------------
  OPEN                info         The word "Open" and an outline dot
  AVAILABLE           info         The word "Available"
  MATCHED             warn         The word "Matched" and a link icon
  CLAIMED             warn         The word "Claimed"
  PENDING             warn         The word "Pending" and a clock icon
  APPROVED            ok           The word "Approved" and a check icon
  RESOLVED / RETURNED ok           The word and a filled check
  REJECTED            err          The word and a cross icon
  EXPIRED / DISPOSED  ink-500      The word, in grey, de-emphasised

  Band                Treatment
  ------------------  ------------------------------------------------------
  STRONG              Brand-600 numeric badge, brand-100 card border, the
                      word "Strong match" in full
  POSSIBLE            Ink-700 numeric badge, ink-200 border, "Possible match"
  WEAK                Ink-500 numeric badge, no border emphasis, "Weak match",
                      collapsed by default

Note that STRONG uses the brand colour rather than green. Green would read as
"confirmed", and a strong match is a suggestion, not a confirmation. The
distinction matters: a user who reads green as certainty is more likely to file a
careless claim.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4 Item Colour Swatches
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The colours table carries a hex value per row precisely so that a swatch can be
rendered. Swatches appear on browse cards, on the report form's colour selector,
and on match cards.

A swatch is a 20-pixel rounded square with a one-pixel --ink-300 border, which is
necessary because a white swatch on a white card is otherwise invisible. The
colour name accompanies the swatch in every context; the swatch is an
accelerator, never the sole label.

================================================================================
4. VISUAL LANGUAGE — TYPOGRAPHY
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Typeface
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A single family, Inter, with the system stack as fallback:

  font-family: Inter, -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Segoe UI",
               Roboto, "Helvetica Neue", Arial, sans-serif;

One family, three weights (400, 500, 700). A second display family was
considered and rejected: it would add a font request, a flash of unstyled text,
and no comprehension benefit in an interface whose longest heading is four
words.

Numerals are set with tabular figures wherever they appear in a column — match
scores, day counts, analytics figures — so that digits align vertically.

  font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums;

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 Type Scale
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A 1.25 ratio scale, truncated to seven steps. More steps invite inconsistency
without adding expressiveness.

  Token        Size    Line height   Weight   Use
  -----------  ------  ------------  -------  ---------------------------
  --t-display  32px    38px          700      Landing headline only
  --t-h1       26px    32px          700      Screen titles
  --t-h2       20px    28px          600      Section headings
  --t-h3       17px    24px          600      Card titles
  --t-body     15px    23px          400      Body text, form values
  --t-small    13px    19px          400      Metadata, helper text
  --t-micro    11px    16px          500      Badges, uppercase labels

Minimum body size is 15 pixels rather than the more common 14. The extra pixel
costs one line of vertical space per paragraph and measurably improves reading
on a phone held at arm's length, which is the actual usage posture.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 Measure and Hierarchy
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Body text is constrained to 68 characters maximum, achieved with a max-width of
34rem on prose containers. Beyond about 75 characters the eye loses the line
return, which is why a full-width paragraph on a desktop monitor is harder to
read than a narrow one.

Hierarchy is established by weight and spacing before it is established by size.
Two adjacent elements at 17px/600 and 15px/400 with 8 pixels between them read as
a clear title-and-detail pair; the same pair at 22px and 15px with no spacing
change reads as noisier.

================================================================================
5. SPACING, LAYOUT AND GRID
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Spacing Scale
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A 4-pixel base with a restricted set of steps. Restriction is the point: a
designer or developer choosing between 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 and 64 will
produce a consistent interface. One choosing freely will not.

  --s-1   4px    Icon-to-label gap
  --s-2   8px    Within a component
  --s-3   12px   Between tightly related elements
  --s-4   16px   Standard element gap, card padding on mobile
  --s-5   24px   Card padding on desktop, between cards
  --s-6   32px   Between sections
  --s-7   48px   Between major regions
  --s-8   64px   Page top and bottom margins on desktop

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2 Grid
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Breakpoint    Width      Columns   Gutter   Margin
  ------------  ---------  --------  -------  --------
  Mobile        < 640px    4         16px     16px
  Tablet        640–1023   8         20px     24px
  Desktop       ≥ 1024px   12        24px     auto, max 1200px

Content max-width is 1200 pixels. Beyond that the page centres rather than
stretching, because a browse grid at 1800 pixels wide produces cards so far apart
that scanning becomes eye movement rather than peripheral recognition.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3 Elevation
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Four levels, used sparingly. Shadows in this design indicate interactive layers,
not decoration.

  --e-0   none                                        Page surfaces
  --e-1   0 1px 2px rgba(20,24,28,0.06)               Cards at rest
  --e-2   0 2px 8px rgba(20,24,28,0.09)               Cards on hover
  --e-3   0 8px 24px rgba(20,24,28,0.14)              Modals, sheets
  --e-4   0 16px 40px rgba(20,24,28,0.18)             Toasts

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.4 Corner Radii
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  --r-sm   4px    Badges, swatches, small chips
  --r-md   8px    Inputs, buttons
  --r-lg   12px   Cards
  --r-xl   16px   Modals and sheets
  --r-full 999px  Pills and avatar circles

================================================================================
6. COMPONENT LIBRARY
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Button
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Variants:  primary | secondary | ghost | danger
  Sizes:     lg (48px) | md (40px) | sm (32px)

  Primary:    --brand-600 background, white text, --r-md
  Secondary:  --ink-000 background, --ink-300 border, --ink-900 text
  Ghost:      transparent, --brand-600 text, underline on hover
  Danger:     --err-600 background, white text

  States:
    default   as specified
    hover     background darkens one step, --e-2 on raised variants
    active    background darkens two steps, transform: translateY(1px)
    focus     3px --brand-100 ring, offset 2px, never removed
    disabled  40% opacity, cursor not-allowed, no hover response
    loading   label replaced by a spinner, width preserved so the button
              does not resize and shift the layout beneath it

  The preserved width during loading is a small detail with a real effect: a
  button that shrinks when clicked moves everything below it, and on a phone
  that can move the next control under the user's thumb mid-tap.

  Minimum tap target is 44×44 pixels regardless of the visual size, achieved
  with padding rather than with dimensions where the visual button is smaller.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.2 Text Input and Textarea
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Structure:  Label (above, always visible)
              Input
              Helper text or error message (below, reserved space)

  Height:     44px for single-line
  Border:     1px --ink-300, --r-md
  Focus:      border --brand-600, 3px --brand-100 ring
  Error:      border --err-600, message in --err-600 at --t-small
  Disabled:   --ink-100 background

Labels sit above the field and remain visible. Placeholder-as-label was rejected
outright: the label vanishes when the user starts typing, which means anyone
interrupted mid-form must clear the field to remember what it was for. This is a
well-documented usability failure and it appears in a great many student
projects.

Helper text occupies reserved vertical space whether or not a message is
present, so that the appearance of an error does not shift the layout.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.3 Select and Searchable Select
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Plain select for lists under about a dozen options: categories, colours,
custody.

Searchable select for locations, which number around eighty. Behaviour:

  - Click opens a panel with a text filter focused
  - Typing filters, matching on location name and building
  - Options grouped by building with sticky group headers
  - Arrow keys move, Enter selects, Escape closes
  - The selected value displays as "Reading Hall — Central Library"

The grouping by building is what makes eighty options navigable. Ungrouped, the
list is a wall; grouped, the user finds their building in one glance and then
picks within five options.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.4 Colour Swatch Selector
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A wrapping row of circular swatches, 36 pixels, each with its colour name
beneath at --t-micro.

  default   1px --ink-300 border
  selected  3px --brand-600 ring plus a check mark, in white or ink depending
            on the swatch's luminance
  focus     3px --brand-100 ring

The check mark is essential. A selected swatch indicated only by a ring is
ambiguous at a glance and invisible to anyone with a colour vision deficiency
when the ring colour and the swatch colour are close.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.5 Item Card
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The most-used component in the product.

  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ ●white   AirPods                       [›] │   ← swatch, name, chevron
  │          Electronics · Apple               │   ← category · brand
  │          Central Library · 11 Sep          │   ← where · when
  │          ┌──────────────┐                  │
  │          │ 3 days here  │                  │   ← age chip
  │          └──────────────┘                  │
  └────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  Padding:     --s-4 mobile, --s-5 desktop
  Radius:      --r-lg
  Border:      1px --ink-200
  Shadow:      --e-1 at rest, --e-2 on hover
  Hover:       border --ink-300, translateY(-1px)
  Whole card is the click target, not just the title

The age chip earns its place by creating gentle urgency. An item that has been
waiting eleven days is more likely to be looked at carefully than one with no
temporal context.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.6 Match Card
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The single most important component in the product, and the only place the
design is permitted to be emphatic.

  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  ┌────┐                                          │
  │  │ 98 │  STRONG MATCH                            │
  │  └────┘                                          │
  │  ─────────────────────────────────────────────── │
  │  YOUR REPORT              THE FOUND ITEM         │
  │  AirPods Pro              AirPods                │
  │  Central Library          Central Library        │
  │  10 September             11 September           │
  │  ─────────────────────────────────────────────── │
  │  ✓ Same category (Electronics)            +20    │
  │  ✓ Same brand (Apple)                     +20    │
  │  ✓ Same location (Central Library)        +20    │
  │  ✓ Same colour (White)                    +15    │
  │  ✓ Found 1 day after you lost it          +13    │
  │  ✓ Shared keywords: black, case           +10    │
  │  ─────────────────────────────────────────────── │
  │  [ This is mine ]              [ Not mine ]      │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  Score badge:  56×56, --r-md, --brand-600 background, white, --t-h1,
                tabular numerals
  Band label:   --t-micro, uppercase, 0.06em letter-spacing
  Card border:  2px --brand-100 for STRONG, 1px --ink-200 otherwise
  Reason rows:  checkmark icon, label left, points right-aligned, tabular

Two design rules govern this component and both are non-negotiable.

First, the reason points are right-aligned in a column with tabular numerals so
that the user can visually add them and arrive at the badge number. The interface
is inviting the user to check its arithmetic, and it must therefore make the
arithmetic checkable.

Second, the points displayed are the points stored in matches.reason_json. The
client performs no computation. If a weight is retuned next term, a match scored
under the old weights still displays the breakdown that produced its score.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.7 Status Badge
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Height 22px, padding 0 8px, --r-full, --t-micro uppercase,
  600 weight, 0.04em letter-spacing.
  Background: semantic -100 token. Text: semantic -600 token.
  A 6px dot precedes the label: outlined for in-progress states, filled for
  terminal states.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.8 Timeline
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ●───  10 Sep, 18:42   Report created                Ananya
  │                     Status set to Open
  │
  ●───  11 Sep, 09:15   Match suggested               System
  │                     Match #302 · score 98 · Strong
  │
  ●───  11 Sep, 14:20   Claim approved                Rahul
  │                     Open → Matched
  │
  ●───  12 Sep, 10:07   Item collected                Ananya
                        Matched → Resolved

  Rail:   2px --ink-200 vertical line
  Node:   10px circle, semantic colour matching the resulting status,
          white 2px ring so it reads as sitting on the rail
  Last node is filled and slightly larger

The status transition is rendered as "Open → Matched" rather than as two
separate values, because the arrow is what makes it a history rather than a list
of facts.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.9 Empty State
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Structure:  Illustration or icon, 64px, --ink-300
              Headline, --t-h3, --ink-900
              Explanation, --t-body, --ink-700, max 42 characters per line
              Primary action
              Optional secondary action

  Vertical centring within the available region, max-width 400px.

Every empty state names a cause and offers an action. "No results" alone is
banned by review.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.10 Modal and Bottom Sheet
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Desktop: centred modal, max-width 480px, --r-xl, --e-3, scrim
rgba(20,24,28,0.45).
Mobile: bottom sheet, full width, top corners --r-xl, drag handle, slides up.

Behaviour: focus trapped within, Escape closes, scrim click closes for
non-destructive modals only, focus returns to the triggering element on close,
background scroll locked.

Destructive confirmations do not close on scrim click, because an accidental
tap outside a confirmation should not dismiss it in either direction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.11 Toast
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Bottom-centre on mobile, bottom-right on desktop. Auto-dismiss after four
seconds, or six seconds when an undo action is present. Maximum two stacked;
further toasts queue. Dismissible manually. Never used for errors that require a
decision — those are inline or modal, because a toast that disappears takes its
information with it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.12 Skeleton Loader
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Shapes match the content that will replace them: a card skeleton has a swatch
circle, three text bars of decreasing width, and a chip. A shimmer sweeps at
1.4s. Skeletons appear only after 300 milliseconds of waiting, so a fast response
never flashes one.

================================================================================
8. INTERACTION DESIGN
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 Form Interaction
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Validation timing. Required-field validation fires on blur, never on keystroke —
validating as someone types tells them their half-entered email is invalid, which
is both true and useless. Format validation fires on blur. Cross-field validation
fires on submit. Once a field has shown an error, it revalidates on change, so
the user sees the error clear as soon as they fix it.

Error summary. On a failed submit, the page scrolls to the first invalid field
and focuses it. A summary at the top lists every error as a link. This matters on
a long form where the invalid field may be off-screen.

Autosave. The two report forms mirror their state to session storage on every
change, debounced at 500 milliseconds. An accidental refresh or a back-navigation
restores the draft, with a dismissible banner: "We restored your draft."

Draft expiry. Session storage entries carry a timestamp and are discarded after
24 hours, so an abandoned draft does not pre-fill an unrelated report a week
later.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.2 Smart Defaults
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Date defaults to today, which is correct for the overwhelming majority of
reports.

Category is inferred from the item name by a keyword map held client-side:
"airpod|earphone|earbud|headphone" → Electronics, "bottle|flask" → Water Bottle,
"id|card" → Identity Document, and so on. The inference is always overridable
and is annotated with the "Chosen from what you typed" helper line so the user
understands where the selection came from and does not feel the form is
guessing at them.

Location remembers the last value used by this user in this session, which helps
the staff console enormously and helps students slightly.

Colour and brand are never pre-selected, because a wrong pre-selection on an
optional field is worse than an empty one: it introduces false data into the
match score.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.3 Progressive Disclosure
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The storage-location field on S07 appears only when custody is not "I still have
it". The free-text brand field appears only when brand is "Other". The custom
date range on S09 appears only when the custom radio is selected. Weak matches
on S13 are collapsed behind a labelled control with a count.

In every case the hidden content is genuinely conditional. Progressive
disclosure applied to fields that are always relevant just hides things.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.4 Feedback Latency
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Under 100ms     Feels instantaneous. No feedback needed.
  100–300ms       Perceptible. Button enters pressed state only.
  300ms–1s        Skeleton or inline spinner.
  1s–3s           Skeleton plus a message naming what is happening.
  Over 3s         Progress indication and, where possible, partial results.

The claim approval action sits in the 300ms–1s band and shows a button spinner.
The report submission sits in the same band but transitions to a new screen, so
the transition itself is the feedback.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.5 Undo
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Match dismissal is the only undoable action, offered for ten seconds via a toast.
Everything else that is destructive is confirmed instead.

The rule applied: an action is undoable if it is easy to trigger accidentally and
its consequence is invisible. Dismissing a match on a phone qualifies on both
counts. Approving a claim does not — it is hard to trigger accidentally because
it is confirmed, and its consequence is highly visible to two people.

================================================================================
9. MOTION AND TRANSITIONS
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.1 Durations and Easing
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  --motion-fast    120ms   Hover, focus, small state changes
  --motion-base    200ms   Most transitions
  --motion-slow    320ms   Modals, sheets, route changes

  --ease-out       cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)      entering
  --ease-in        cubic-bezier(0.7, 0, 0.84, 0)      exiting
  --ease-in-out    cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)     moving

Entering elements use ease-out, which starts fast and settles, so content feels
like it arrives. Exiting elements use ease-in, which accelerates away, so
dismissal feels decisive.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.2 Specific Motions
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Page transition       Opacity 0→1 over 200ms with a 6px upward translate.
                        No horizontal slide, which reads as navigation
                        direction and is wrong for a shallow app.
  Modal enter           Scrim fades 200ms; dialog scales 0.96→1 with opacity
                        over 200ms ease-out.
  Bottom sheet          Translates from 100% to 0 over 320ms ease-out.
  Card hover            translateY(-1px) and shadow e-1→e-2 over 120ms.
  Skeleton shimmer      1.4s linear infinite.
  Toast                 Slides 12px up with opacity over 200ms.
  Match card reveal     On the confirmation screen, the strong-match card
                        fades and scales in over 320ms with a 120ms delay.
                        This is the one piece of deliberately expressive
                        motion in the product, and it exists because that
                        moment is the product's payoff.
  Dismiss               Card scales to 0.96 and fades over 200ms, then the
                        list reflows over 200ms.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.3 Reduced Motion
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
    *, *::before, *::after {
      animation-duration: 0.01ms !important;
      transition-duration: 0.01ms !important;
    }
  }

Opacity transitions are retained at a shortened duration, because an instant
appearance is more jarring than a fast fade. Transforms and scale effects are
removed entirely, since those are what trigger vestibular discomfort.

================================================================================
10. RESPONSIVE DESIGN
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.1 Approach
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Mobile-first. Base styles target the smallest viewport and min-width media
queries add capability. This ordering matters more than it sounds: designing
desktop-first and subtracting produces mobile screens that are compromises,
whereas designing mobile-first and adding produces desktop screens that are
enhancements.

The expected traffic split is heavily mobile. A student reports a loss from
wherever they realised it, which is almost never at a desk.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.2 Per-Screen Adaptation
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Screen   Mobile                       Desktop
  -------  ---------------------------  ---------------------------------
  S01      Stacked, full-width actions  Two-column hero with the recent
                                        strip beside the actions
  S04      Single column cards          Two columns: reports left,
                                        attention items right
  S06/S07  Single column, sticky        Two columns for short fields,
           submit                       full width for textareas
  S09      1-col grid, filter sheet     3-col grid, persistent filter rail
  S13      Stacked cards                2-col card grid
  S14      Panels stacked, yours first  Side-by-side panels
  S19      Full width, stacked quotes   Centred, max 560px, quotes stacked
  S22–S27  Card lists                   Tables

The S14 stacking order on mobile — the user's own report first, then the found
item — is deliberate. The user needs to re-anchor on what they lost before
evaluating what was found.

Tables becoming card lists on mobile rather than scrolling horizontally is the
single most important responsive decision in the staff and admin sections. A
horizontally scrolling table on a phone is technically responsive and practically
unusable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.3 Touch Considerations
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Minimum tap target 44×44 pixels with at least 8 pixels between adjacent targets.
Primary actions positioned within thumb reach in the lower two-thirds of the
screen. Destructive actions positioned away from primary ones, never adjacent.
No hover-dependent information anywhere, since hover does not exist on touch —
the "see all reasons" affordance is a tap, not a hover.

================================================================================
11. ACCESSIBILITY
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11.1 Target
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WCAG 2.1 Level AA.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11.2 Colour and Contrast
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  --ink-900 on --ink-000    15.8:1   ✓ AAA
  --ink-700 on --ink-000     9.1:1   ✓ AAA
  --ink-500 on --ink-000     4.6:1   ✓ AA
  white on --brand-600       6.4:1   ✓ AA
  --err-600 on --err-100     6.0:1   ✓ AA
  --ok-600 on --ok-100       5.4:1   ✓ AA

Non-text contrast: all input borders, focus rings and swatch outlines meet 3:1
against their adjacent colours.

Colour is never the only carrier of meaning. Status badges carry a word and a
dot shape. Match bands carry a numeral and a label. Item colours carry a name.
Validation errors carry an icon and a message.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11.3 Keyboard
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every interactive element is reachable by Tab in a logical order matching the
visual order. Focus is never removed, only restyled. A skip-to-content link is
the first focusable element on every page.

Modals trap focus and restore it on close. The searchable select supports arrow
navigation, Enter to select, Escape to close, and Home/End to jump. The swatch
selector is a radio group navigable with arrows.

Escape closes any overlay. Enter submits any form from any field except a
textarea.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11.4 Screen Readers
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Semantic HTML first: nav, main, section, h1 through h3 in order without skipping
levels, button for actions, a for navigation. ARIA is used only where semantics
are insufficient.

  - Every input has an associated label element, not a placeholder or an
    aria-label where a visible label exists
  - Error messages are linked with aria-describedby and the field carries
    aria-invalid
  - The item count region on browse is aria-live="polite" so filter changes
    are announced
  - Toasts are role="status" for informational and role="alert" for errors
  - The match score is announced as "Match score 98 out of 100, strong match"
    via an aria-label on the badge, since "98" alone is meaningless
  - Skeletons are aria-hidden with an aria-live region announcing "Loading"
  - Icon-only controls carry an aria-label; the chevron on cards is
    aria-hidden because the card itself is the labelled control

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11.5 Content Accessibility
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Reading level targeted at a general audience, not a technical one. Sentences
average under 20 words. Error messages state the correction, not the failure.
Link text is meaningful out of context: "See the match" rather than "Click
here".

================================================================================
12. CONTENT AND VOICE
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12.1 Voice
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Plain, direct, warm without being chirpy. The product is helping with a small
frustration, not celebrating a triumph. Exclamation marks are reserved for the
single success moment at handover completion, and even there they are optional.

Second person for the user, first person plural for the system: "We will check
every item found from now on." The first person plural is a deliberate choice —
it makes the system an agent doing work on the user's behalf, which is exactly
the standing-query model the product needs the user to internalise.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12.2 Vocabulary Standards
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Use                      Not
  -----------------------  ----------------------------------
  report                   listing, post, entry, record
  match                    result, hit, suggestion, candidate
  claim                    request, application
  found item               item, product, article
  the finder               the reporter, the user
  Security Desk            security, the desk, lost property

Consistency here is not pedantry. A user who reads "report" on one screen and
"listing" on the next has to work out whether they are the same thing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12.3 Message Patterns
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Success      What happened, then what is next.
               "Your report is live. We will check every item found from now
                on against your description."

  Error        What happened, then how to fix it.
               "That date is in the future. Choose a date on or before today."

  Empty        Why it is empty, then what would change it.
               "No items match these filters. Try widening the date range."

  Confirm      What will happen, then whether it can be undone.
               "Withdraw this report? It will stop matching against new
                finds. You can file a new report later."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12.4 The Messages That Matter Most
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three strings carry disproportionate weight and are worth writing and rewriting.

The no-match confirmation, because it determines retention:
  "Your report is live. Nothing matching has been found yet. From now on,
   every item anyone reports finding will be checked against your description
   automatically. We will tell you the moment something matches."

The private field explanation, because it determines whether verification works:
  "Never shown to anyone. We use it to check that a claimant really owns the
   item."

The concurrency conflict, because it must not read as a bug:
  "Someone else's claim on this item was approved a moment ago. Nothing has
   changed here."

================================================================================
13. ICONOGRAPHY AND IMAGERY
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13.1 Icons
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A single open-source outline set, 1.5px stroke, 24px default grid, rendered at
16, 20 and 24 pixels. Icons are never the sole label on a control that a novice
must understand; a bare icon is acceptable only for universally understood
actions such as close and back.

  Category icons: earphone, laptop, book, bag, wallet, card, key, bottle,
                  shirt, umbrella, glasses, ball, flask, box (fallback)
  Action icons:   plus, search, filter, close, chevron, check, alert, clock,
                  lock, eye, undo, arrow-right

Category icons appear on browse cards and in the category selector. Their
purpose is to accelerate scanning, so the set is drawn at one weight with
consistent visual mass; a set mixing filled and outline styles defeats the
purpose.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13.2 Imagery Policy
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

No photographs in version 1.0. The MVP has no image upload, and stock imagery
would add page weight for no informational value.

The colour swatch is the visual identity of an item. This is a constraint turned
into a feature: a wall of coloured dots is scannable in a way that a wall of
identical placeholder thumbnails would not be.

When image upload arrives as a post-MVP enhancement, the design rules are
already set: 4:3 aspect, object-fit cover, maximum three per item, a blurred
low-quality placeholder while loading, and no images at all for sensitive
categories.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13.3 Illustration
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Empty states use a single-line illustration at 64 pixels in --ink-300 — a
magnifying glass for no results, an open box for no items, a check for nothing
pending. Monochrome and small, so they read as punctuation rather than as
decoration.
