1. PURPOSE AND READING GUIDE
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 What This Document Is
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This document describes how a person moves through FoundIt: which screen they
land on, what they see, what they can do, what the system does in response, and
where they end up. It is the behavioural specification that sits between the
product requirements, which say what must be possible, and the design
specification, which says what it looks like.

Every flow is described at three levels simultaneously. The user level says what
the person perceives and decides. The system level says what the application
does behind that perception. The data level says which relations are read and
written and inside what transaction boundary. Keeping all three visible in one
document is the point: a flow that reads well but writes badly is not a good
flow, and a flow that writes correctly but leaves the user confused about
whether anything happened is not a good flow either.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2 Notation
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  [SCREEN]        A distinct route with its own URL
  (modal)         An overlay that does not change the route
  → action        A user-initiated transition
  ⇒ system        An automatic transition following system work
  ◆ decision      A branch point
  ▸ DB            A database operation, annotated with its transaction
  ✱ guard         A precondition that must hold or the transition is blocked

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3 The Two Journeys That Matter
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Everything in this document serves one of two journeys, and when a design
decision is contested, it is resolved by asking which journey it serves better.

The owner journey: I lost something → I report it → the system finds a candidate
→ I prove it is mine → I get it back.

The finder journey: I found something → I report it → somebody claims it → I
check them → I hand it over.

Every other flow — browse, search, history, analytics, staff intake — is
supporting apparatus. If the two core journeys are slow or confusing, no amount
of polish elsewhere rescues the product.

2. NAVIGATION MODEL AND SCREEN INVENTORY
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Structure
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FoundIt is a shallow application. The deepest path from the landing screen to
any action is three levels, and most actions are reachable in two. Shallowness
is chosen deliberately: a person who has just lost something is not in a mood to
navigate.

  Level 0   Landing / Dashboard
  Level 1   Report Lost | Report Found | Browse | My Reports | My Claims
  Level 2   Match list | Item detail | Claim detail | Verification queue
  Level 3   Timeline | Claim submission modal

Persistent navigation is a top bar on desktop and a bottom bar on mobile,
carrying five destinations: Home, Browse, Report (a compound action button), My
Items, and Profile. The Report control is visually dominant because reporting is
the action the product most wants to encourage.

3. ENTRY POINTS AND FIRST-RUN EXPERIENCE
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Entry Points
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Cold entry. The person types the URL or follows a link from a poster or a
messaging group. They arrive at S01 unauthenticated.

Returning entry. A valid session token exists. The router bypasses S01 entirely
and lands on S04. There is no reason to show a marketing page to somebody who
already has an account, and doing so is a small but real friction tax on the
users who matter most.

Deep entry. A link to a specific found item, shared by someone who thinks they
recognise it. The route is S10. If unauthenticated, the person is sent to S03
with a return path parameter, and after signing in is delivered to S10 rather
than to the dashboard. Losing the destination on sign-in is one of the most
common and most annoying defects in web applications, and it is avoided by
carrying the return path through the authentication flow explicitly.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 S01 — Landing
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The landing screen does three jobs and no others: it says what the product is in
one line, it shows the two primary actions, and it demonstrates that the system
is alive.

  Content:
    - Product name and one-line description
    - Two large actions: "I lost something" and "I found something"
    - A live counter: items currently listed, items returned this month
    - A strip of the six most recent found items, redacted to category,
      colour swatch, building and date
    - Secondary link to sign in

The live counter and the recent strip exist to address risk RK-1 from the PRD.
A landing page that says "report your lost item" against an empty void gives no
reason to believe the system works. A landing page that says "148 items
currently listed, 37 returned this month" and shows six of them does.

  → "I lost something"   ⇒ if authenticated, S06; else S03 with return=/report/lost
  → "I found something"  ⇒ if authenticated, S07; else S03 with return=/report/found
  → "Sign in"            ⇒ S03

Note that the person states their intent before authenticating. This ordering
matters: intent-first flows convert better than authentication-first flows,
because the person has already invested a decision by the time they are asked to
create an account.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3 S04 — Dashboard, First Visit
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A newly registered user's dashboard has nothing in it. The empty state carries
the entire weight of explaining the product, so it is written carefully:

  "You have no reports yet.
   If you have lost something, report it and we will check every item that
   gets found from now on against your description.
   If you have found something, report it and we will look for the owner."

  → Report lost   ⇒ S06
  → Report found  ⇒ S07
  → Browse        ⇒ S09

The phrase "from now on" is doing specific work. It sets the expectation that a
report is a standing query rather than a one-time search, which is the mental
model the product needs the user to hold. Without it, a user who reports a loss
and sees no matches concludes the system has failed.

4. FLOW A — REGISTRATION AND AUTHENTICATION
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Registration — S02
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Fields:  Full name          required
           Email              required, institutional domain
           Password           required, minimum 8 characters
           Confirm password   required, must match
           Phone              optional
           Department         optional, select from list

  → Submit
      ✱ client validation: all required present, passwords match, email shape
      ▸ DB  BEGIN
              INSERT INTO users (...) VALUES (...)
              INSERT INTO audit_log ('USER', new id, 'CREATED', ...)
            COMMIT
      ◆ 23505 unique_violation on uq_users_email
          ⇒ stay on S02, field-level error on email:
            "An account already exists with this email. Sign in instead?"
            with a link that carries the email through to S03
      ◆ domain not permitted
          ⇒ field-level error naming the accepted domain
      ◆ success
          ⇒ issue token, redirect to return path or S04

The duplicate-email path deserves comment because it is where a careless
implementation leaks information or wastes the user's time. FoundIt tells the
user the account exists, which is an acceptable disclosure in a closed community
where email addresses are already institutional and enumerable, and offers the
sign-in link pre-filled. A public product would handle this differently; this
one does not need to.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 Sign In — S03
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Fields:  Email, Password
  → Submit
      ▸ DB  SELECT user_id, password_hash, role, is_active
              FROM users WHERE lower(email) = lower($1)
      ◆ no row, or bcrypt comparison fails
          ⇒ single generic error: "Email or password is incorrect."
            The same message for both cases, and the same response time,
            achieved by running a dummy bcrypt comparison when no row is found.
            Distinguishing the two would confirm which emails are registered.
      ◆ is_active false
          ⇒ "This account is not active. Contact the help desk."
      ◆ success
          ⇒ issue token, redirect to return path or S04
      ◆ sixth failed attempt within 15 minutes
          ⇒ 429, "Too many attempts. Try again in a few minutes."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 Session Behaviour
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The access token lives one hour. A silent refresh happens on any API call made
within the last ten minutes of validity, so an active user is never interrupted.
A user returning after the refresh window is sent to S03 with the current route
as the return path, so they resume exactly where they were.

Sign-out revokes the refresh token server-side and clears client storage.

5. FLOW B — REPORTING A LOST ITEM
================================================================================

This is the highest-traffic flow and the one with the tightest time budget: the
PRD requires completion in under ninety seconds by a first-time user.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 S06 — The Form
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The form is presented as a single scrolling page rather than a multi-step
wizard. Wizards feel more guided but cost a page transition per step and hide
the total length, which raises abandonment. Six fields on one screen is not
enough length to justify chunking.

  Field order and rationale:

  1. What did you lose?        Text, required
                               First because it is the thing the person is
                               already thinking about. Autocomplete suggests
                               common item names from previous reports.

  2. Category                  Select, required
                               Pre-selected by a keyword lookup against the
                               item name: typing "airpods" selects Electronics
                               automatically, and the user may override.

  3. Where?                    Searchable select, required
                               Grouped by building. A type-ahead filters the
                               eighty-odd locations to a handful in two
                               keystrokes.

  4. When?                     Date, required, defaults to today
                               A future date is blocked in the picker, so the
                               CHECK constraint is a backstop rather than the
                               primary experience.

  5. Colour                    Swatch selector, optional
                               Rendered as colour chips rather than a dropdown,
                               because recognition is faster than reading.

  6. Brand                     Select with free-text escape, optional

  7. Anything distinctive?     Textarea, optional, 300 characters
                               Placeholder: "A scratch, a sticker, what was
                               inside, a name written on it..."

  → Submit
      ✱ guard: required fields present; lost_date <= today
      ▸ DB  BEGIN
              INSERT INTO lost_items (...) RETURNING lost_id
              SELECT generate_matches_for_lost(lost_id)
              INSERT INTO audit_log ('LOST_ITEM', lost_id, 'CREATED',
                                     NULL, 'OPEN', user_id, NULL)
            COMMIT
      ⇒ S08 with the match count and top match carried in the response

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2 Why Matching Runs Inside the Creation Transaction
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three alternatives were considered.

Asynchronous, after commit. The report is created, a job is queued, matches
appear a few seconds later. This is the scalable choice and the wrong one here.
It means the confirmation screen cannot say anything about matches, so the user
must either wait on a spinner or be sent to a screen that says "we will check
and let you know", which is exactly the vague non-answer the product exists to
replace.

Synchronous but outside the transaction. The report commits, then matching runs
and commits separately. A failure between the two leaves a report with no
matches and no record that matching was attempted, and the user sees an empty
list with no explanation.

Synchronous inside the transaction. The report and its matches commit together
or not at all. The confirmation screen can state the outcome immediately. The
cost is transaction duration, budgeted at 200 milliseconds in NFR-1.1 and
measured.

The third is chosen. The reasoning is worth articulating because it is a case
where the textbook answer, asynchronous processing, is the wrong answer for the
product, and understanding why is more valuable than reciting the pattern.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3 S08 — Confirmation, Three Variants
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The confirmation screen branches on what matching found, and the three variants
are written to produce three different, appropriate emotional responses.

  ◆ At least one STRONG match
      "Good news. We may have already found it."
      The top match is rendered as a full card with its score and reasons.
      Primary action: "See the match"  ⇒ S14
      Secondary: "See all N matches"   ⇒ S13

  ◆ Only POSSIBLE or WEAK matches
      "Your report is live. We found N items that might be yours."
      Rendered as a compact list, ranked.
      Primary action: "Review them"    ⇒ S13
      Secondary: "Browse everything"   ⇒ S09

  ◆ No matches above the floor
      "Your report is live.
       Nothing matching has been found yet. From now on, every item anyone
       reports finding will be checked against your description automatically.
       We will tell you the moment something matches."
      Primary action: "Browse found items"  ⇒ S09
      Secondary: "Back to my reports"       ⇒ S11

The third variant is the one that determines whether the product retains users,
and it is the one most likely to be written carelessly. "No matches found" is a
failure message. The version above is a status message that explains the
standing-query model and directs the user to the one thing they can usefully do
next.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.4 Editing and Withdrawing
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

From S12, a report in status OPEN offers Edit and Withdraw.

  → Edit
      ✱ guard: status = 'OPEN' and viewer is the owner
      ⇒ S06 pre-populated
      → Save
          ▸ DB  BEGIN
                  UPDATE lost_items SET ... WHERE lost_id = $1 AND user_id = $2
                  SELECT generate_matches_for_lost($1)   -- re-score
                  INSERT INTO audit_log (..., 'UPDATED', ...)
                COMMIT
          Note: re-running the match procedure is essential. A user who corrects
          the location from the wrong building to the right one has materially
          changed the match landscape, and stale scores would be worse than no
          scores.

  → Withdraw
      (modal) "Withdraw this report? It will stop matching against new finds.
               You can file a new report later."
      → Confirm
          ▸ DB  BEGIN
                  UPDATE lost_items SET status='CLOSED_BY_USER' WHERE ...
                  UPDATE matches SET status='INVALIDATED'
                    WHERE lost_id=$1 AND status='SUGGESTED'
                  INSERT INTO audit_log (..., 'STATUS_CHANGED', 'OPEN',
                                         'CLOSED_BY_USER', ...)
                COMMIT
          ⇒ S11

Matches are invalidated rather than deleted, preserving the record that a
suggestion was made. This is consistent with the general principle that the
system forgets nothing.

6. FLOW C — REPORTING A FOUND ITEM
================================================================================

The found flow is deliberately shorter than the lost flow, because finder
motivation is weaker and decays with friction. Every field on this form has
earned its place.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 S07 — The Form
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. What did you find?        Text, required
  2. Category                  Select, required, keyword pre-selection
  3. Where did you find it?    Searchable select, required
  4. When?                     Date, required, defaults to today
  5. Colour                    Swatch, optional
  6. Brand                     Select, optional
  7. Public description        Textarea, optional
                               Labelled "What will help the owner recognise
                               it?" with a note: "This is shown publicly."
  8. Private detail            Textarea, REQUIRED
                               Labelled "What detail would only the owner
                               know?" with a note: "This is never shown to
                               anyone. We use it to check that a claimant
                               really owns the item."
  9. Where is it now?          Radio, required
  10. Is this valuable?        Checkbox, optional, default unchecked
                               "This looks valuable — please have
                                staff handle the verification."
                               ( ) I still have it
                               ( ) I left it at the Security Desk
                               ( ) I left it at a department office
                               ( ) I left it at a hostel office
                               Selecting anything other than the first reveals
                               a storage-location text field, required.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.2 The Two Description Fields
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Splitting description into public and private is the single most important
interaction decision in the product, and it is also the one most likely to
confuse a user who is not paying attention. The interface therefore makes the
distinction unmissable: the two fields are visually separated, differently
coloured, and each carries an explicit visibility note. The private field is
marked with a lock icon and the words "Never shown publicly" directly beneath
the input, not in a tooltip.

The private field is required at the database level (found_items.private_feature
NOT NULL) because a found report without it cannot support verification. The
form communicates this as "Required — this is how we protect the item" rather
than as a bare asterisk, because a user who understands why a field is required
supplies a better answer.

Validation rejects a private detail that duplicates the public description
verbatim, with the message: "This is the same as your public description. Please
add something only the owner would know."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.3 Submission
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  → Submit
      ✱ guard: required fields; found_date <= today; storage location present
               when custody is not WITH_FINDER; private detail differs from
               public description
      ▸ DB  BEGIN
              INSERT INTO found_items (...) RETURNING found_id
              SELECT generate_matches_for_found(found_id)
              INSERT INTO audit_log ('FOUND_ITEM', found_id, 'CREATED',
                                     NULL, 'AVAILABLE', user_id, NULL)
            COMMIT
      ⇒ S08

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.4 S08 for a Found Report
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The confirmation differs from the lost variant in a crucial way: it must tell
the finder what to do with the physical object.

  ◆ Category is sensitive (identity documents) or high value
      "Thank you. Please hand this in at the Security Desk, Main Gate.
       Identity documents and valuables are held there so the owner can
       collect them safely. Your report will still find the owner."
      The instruction is prominent and is repeated in the report detail view
      until custody is updated.

  ◆ Ordinary category, finder retains custody
      "Thank you. Keep the item safe. If someone claims it, we will ask you
       one question to check they are the owner, and only then will we share
       contact details."

  ◆ Ordinary category, deposited
      "Thank you. We have recorded that it is at <storage location>."

  ◆ Matching found candidates
      "We have also found N people who reported losing something similar.
       They will see your report now."
      Deliberately, the finder is NOT shown the lost reporters' details and is
      not asked to act. The next move belongs to the owner.

7. FLOW D — BROWSING AND SEARCHING
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 S09 — Browse
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ▸ DB  SELECT ... FROM v_public_found_items
         WHERE (filters)
         ORDER BY found_date DESC
         LIMIT 24 OFFSET $n

  Layout: a responsive card grid, three columns on desktop, one on mobile.
  Each card:  colour swatch | item name | category | building | date
              | "N days here"

  Filter rail (desktop) / filter sheet (mobile):
    Category      multi-select
    Building      multi-select
    Colour        swatch multi-select
    Date range    last 7 / 30 / 90 days / custom
    Search        keyword, debounced 300 ms

  → Apply filter
      Filters are written to the URL query string, so a filtered view is
      shareable and survives a refresh. This is a small thing that users
      notice only by its absence.

  → Keyword search
      ▸ DB  the full-text query Q9, ranked by ts_rank

  → Card click  ⇒ S10

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.2 Pagination
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Server-side, page size 24. The first page is loaded on route entry; subsequent
pages load on an explicit "Load more" action rather than on infinite scroll.
Infinite scroll was rejected because it makes the footer unreachable and because
it hides the total count, and the total count ("148 items") is one of the
reassurance signals the product depends on.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.3 S10 — Found Item Detail
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Shows: item name, category, colour swatch, brand, public description,
         building (and specific location if the category is not sensitive),
         date found, days in system, custody status in general terms
         ("held at the Security Desk" or "with the finder").

  Does NOT show: the private detail, the finder's name, the finder's contact
         details, the exact storage location.

  ◆ The viewer has an OPEN lost report that matches this item
      A banner appears: "This may match your report: <item name>."
      → "Claim this item"  ⇒ S15 for the relevant match

  ◆ The viewer has no matching report
      → "This is mine"
        ⇒ (modal) "To claim an item you need a lost report on file, so we
                   can check the details. File one now — it takes a minute."
        → "File a report"  ⇒ S06, pre-filled with this item's category,
                              location and colour

The requirement that a claim be routed through a match, rather than made
directly against a found item, is a structural protection. It forces the
claimant to commit to a description of the item before they can claim it, and
the description they commit to is recorded. A claimant who describes the item
differently from the listing they are looking at has made a verifiable mistake.

8. FLOW E — REVIEWING MATCHES
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 S13 — Match List for One Report
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ▸ DB  SELECT ... FROM v_match_detail
         WHERE lost_id = $1 AND owner_id = $2 AND status = 'SUGGESTED'
         ORDER BY match_score DESC

  Grouping:
    STRONG MATCHES        rendered as full cards, expanded
    POSSIBLE MATCHES      rendered as compact cards
    ▸ Show N weaker matches   collapsed by default, expands on click

  Each card shows: the found item's public summary, the score as a numeric
  badge with a band label, and the top three reasons inline with a "see all
  reasons" affordance.

  → Card click     ⇒ S14
  → Dismiss (×)    (modal) "Not your item? We will stop showing it."
                   ▸ DB  UPDATE matches SET status='REJECTED_BY_USER'
                          WHERE match_id=$1
                        INSERT INTO audit_log ('MATCH', $1, 'STATUS_CHANGED',
                                               'SUGGESTED','REJECTED_BY_USER',
                                               user_id, NULL)
                   The card animates out. An undo affordance persists for ten
                   seconds, because dismissal is easy to do accidentally and
                   irreversible dismissal of a correct match is the worst
                   outcome the flow can produce.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.2 S14 — Match Detail
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This screen is where the product's central claim — that the database found this
and can explain why — is either made convincing or not.

  Layout: two panels side by side on desktop, stacked on mobile.

    LEFT: YOUR REPORT                RIGHT: THE FOUND ITEM
    AirPods Pro                      AirPods
    Electronics                      Electronics
    Apple                            Apple
    White                            White
    Central Library                  Central Library
    10 September                     11 September
    "Black case, small scratch"      "White earphones in a black case"

  Attributes that agree are visually linked across the two panels. Attributes
  that differ are shown without emphasis rather than being marked in red,
  because a difference is not an error.

  Beneath, the score breakdown:

    Same category (Electronics)                            +20
    Same brand (Apple)                                     +20
    Same location (Central Library)                        +20
    Same colour (White)                                    +15
    Found 1 day after you lost it                          +13
    Shared keywords: black, case                           +10
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    Match score                                             98   STRONG

  The reasons are rendered from matches.reason_json exactly as stored. The
  client performs no arithmetic; it displays the stored total. This guarantees
  that what the user sees is what the engine computed.

  Actions:
    → "This is mine — claim it"   ⇒ S15
    → "Not mine"                  ⇒ dismiss, back to S13
    → "See the item's history"    ⇒ S21

9. FLOW F — CLAIMING AN ITEM

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.1 S15 — Claim Submission
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ✱ guards, checked server-side before the form is even served:
      - the viewer owns the lost item in the match
      - the found item is still AVAILABLE
      - the viewer is not the finder (BR-1, enforced by trg_no_self_claim)
      - the viewer has fewer than MAX_PENDING_CLAIMS pending claims
      - the viewer has not already been rejected three times on this item

  Screen content:

    "Prove it is yours

     The person who found this item told us one detail about it that is not
     shown anywhere on this page. Describe something distinctive about your
     item — a mark, a sticker, an engraving, what was inside it, or anything
     else only the owner would know.

     [                                                          ]
     [                                                          ]
     minimum 10 characters

     The finder will compare your answer with what they told us.
     They will not see your contact details unless they confirm the match."

  → Submit
      ▸ DB  BEGIN
              -- pending claim count check
              SELECT count(*) FROM claims
                WHERE claimant_id=$1 AND status='PENDING';
              INSERT INTO claims (match_id, claimant_id, proof_text, status)
                VALUES ($2, $1, $3, 'PENDING')
                -- trg_claims_denormalise_found sets found_id
                -- trg_no_self_claim guards BR-1
              UPDATE matches SET status='CLAIMED' WHERE match_id=$2;
              INSERT INTO audit_log ('CLAIM', new id, 'CLAIM_CREATED',
                                     NULL, 'PENDING', $1, NULL);
            COMMIT
      ⇒ S17

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.2 Why the Claimant Never Sees the Private Detail
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The obvious alternative design shows the claimant the finder's private detail
and asks "is this your item?". It is obviously wrong, and it is worth saying why
explicitly, because it is the kind of thing that gets built by accident when a
developer is wiring up a screen and the data is already in the response object.

If the claimant sees the detail, the challenge is not a challenge. Anyone can
say yes. The entire verification mechanism reduces to a confirmation dialog.

This is why the privacy boundary is enforced in the view (v_public_found_items
does not contain private_feature) and in the API layer (the claim creation
endpoint's response shape contains no field for it), rather than by remembering
not to render it. A defence that depends on remembering will eventually fail.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.3 S17 — Claim Status
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ◆ status PENDING
      "Waiting for the finder to check your answer.
       We will tell you as soon as they decide."
      A timeline shows: claim filed → awaiting verification.
      → Withdraw claim

  ◆ status APPROVED
      "Confirmed. This item is yours."
      The counterparty's name and contact details appear here, and only here,
      and only now.
      Custody instruction repeated: "Collect it from the Security Desk, Main
      Gate" or "Contact <name> to arrange collection."
      → "I have collected it"  ⇒ S20

  ◆ status REJECTED
      "The finder could not confirm this is your item."
      The rejection reason is shown only if the verifier supplied one and only
      if it does not disclose the private detail; the interface offers the
      verifier a set of neutral canned reasons for exactly this purpose.
      No indication of how close the answer was.
      → "Browse other items"  ⇒ S09

10. FLOW G — VERIFYING A CLAIM
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.1 S18 — Verification Queue
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ▸ DB  SELECT c.claim_id, c.claimed_at, f.item_name, u.full_name
          FROM claims c
          JOIN found_items f ON f.found_id = c.found_id
          JOIN users u ON u.user_id = c.claimant_id
         WHERE c.status = 'PENDING'
           AND ( f.user_id = $1
                 OR ( $2 = 'STAFF' AND EXISTS (
                         SELECT 1 FROM categories cat
                         WHERE cat.category_id = f.category_id
                           AND (cat.requires_staff OR f.is_high_value))))
         ORDER BY c.claimed_at

  The queue shows one row per pending claim with the item, the claimant's
  display name, and how long the claim has been waiting. Claims older than
  seven days are marked.

  → Row click  ⇒ S19

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.2 S19 — The Verification Screen
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This screen is reduced to the smallest thing that supports a correct decision.
The verifier is a student doing a favour, often on a phone, often distracted.

  "Is this their item?

   You said:
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ There is a small scratch on the back of the    │
   │ charging case, near the hinge.                 │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

   They said:
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ The case has a scratch near the hinge on the   │
   │ back, and there's a small blue sticker inside. │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

   [ Yes, it is theirs ]      [ No, it is not ]

   If you are not sure, you can ask the Security Desk to decide instead."

  → "Yes, it is theirs"
      (modal) "Confirm? We will share your contact details with them and mark
               this item as claimed. This cannot be undone without staff help."
      → Confirm
          ▸ DB  the approve_claim() transaction of TRD section 10.2
          ◆ 409 ITEM_NOT_AVAILABLE
              ⇒ "Someone else's claim on this item was approved a moment ago.
                 Nothing has changed here."
                This is the visible face of the concurrency control, and the
                message is written to be comprehensible to a student rather
                than to a database administrator.
          ◆ success
              ⇒ S20

  → "No, it is not"
      (modal) Requires selecting a reason from a fixed list:
              ( ) The detail they gave does not match
              ( ) They gave no useful detail
              ( ) I think this is a different item
              ( ) Other (free text, 300 characters)
      → Confirm
          ▸ DB  BEGIN
                  UPDATE claims SET status='REJECTED', verifier_id=$1,
                         decided_at=NOW(), decision_note=$2
                    WHERE claim_id=$3 AND status='PENDING';
                  UPDATE matches SET status='SUGGESTED'
                    WHERE match_id=(SELECT match_id FROM claims
                                     WHERE claim_id=$3);
                  UPDATE users SET flagged_count = flagged_count + 1
                    WHERE user_id=(SELECT claimant_id FROM claims
                                    WHERE claim_id=$3);
                  INSERT INTO audit_log ('CLAIM', $3, 'CLAIM_REJECTED',
                                         'PENDING','REJECTED', $1, $2);
                COMMIT
          ⇒ S18

  → "Ask the Security Desk to decide"
      ▸ DB  UPDATE claims SET verifier_id = NULL, decision_note='ESCALATED'
            -- routes the claim into the staff queue
      ⇒ S18

The escalation path exists because a verifier forced to choose between two
uncomfortable options will pick one arbitrarily, and an arbitrary approval is
worse than a slow correct one. Giving them a third option costs one row update
and materially improves decision quality.

11. FLOW H — HANDOVER AND COMPLETION
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11.1 S20 — Handover
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The system cannot move a physical object. Its job here is to make the handover
easy to arrange and to record that it happened.

  ◆ custody = DEPOSITED_SECURITY
      "Collect it from the Security Desk, Main Gate.
       Take your student ID. Reference: FI-204."
      The reference code is the found item id formatted for a human, which
      lets the desk staff locate the record without spelling out a description.

  ◆ custody = WITH_FINDER
      Contact details for both parties, and a suggested message the claimant
      can copy:
      "Hi, I am collecting the AirPods you found in the Library on 11 Sept.
       When would suit you?"
      Providing the draft message removes a small social friction that
      measurably delays handovers.

  → "I have collected it" (claimant) or "I have handed it over" (finder)
      ▸ DB  BEGIN
              UPDATE claims SET status='COMPLETED', completed_at=NOW()
                WHERE claim_id=$1 AND status='APPROVED';
              UPDATE found_items SET status='RETURNED', returned_at=NOW()
                WHERE found_id=$2;
              UPDATE lost_items SET status='RESOLVED', resolved_at=NOW()
                WHERE lost_id=$3;
              INSERT INTO audit_log (three rows: claim, found, lost);
            COMMIT
      ⇒ S04 with a success banner: "Returned. That is one more."
      The counter on the landing page increments.

Either party can complete. Requiring both would deadlock whenever one of them
forgets, which is most of the time. A single-party completion is occasionally
premature, and the cost of that is low; the audit trail records who confirmed
it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11.2 Reversal
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Staff only. Available from S22 for any claim in status APPROVED or COMPLETED.

  ▸ DB  BEGIN
          UPDATE claims SET status='REVERSED' WHERE claim_id=$1;
          UPDATE found_items SET status='AVAILABLE', returned_at=NULL
            WHERE found_id=$2;
          UPDATE lost_items SET status='OPEN', resolved_at=NULL
            WHERE lost_id=$3;
          UPDATE matches SET status='SUGGESTED' WHERE found_id=$2
            AND status='INVALIDATED';
          INSERT INTO audit_log (..., 'REVERSED', ...);
        COMMIT

Reversal restores the previous state and leaves the entire history intact, which
is precisely what the audit design exists to make possible. A reversed claim is
visible in the timeline forever.

16. ERROR, EMPTY AND EDGE-CASE FLOWS
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
16.1 Empty States
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every list screen has a designed empty state, and each one explains a cause and
offers an action.

  S04 dashboard, no reports
      "Nothing here yet." + both report actions.

  S09 browse, no items at all
      "No items have been reported found yet. If you found something,
       you could be the first." + report found action.

  S09 browse, filters exclude everything
      "No items match these filters." + "Clear filters" action.
      Critically distinct from the previous message: the first says the system
      is empty, the second says the query is narrow, and conflating them makes
      users think the product is broken.

  S13 matches, none
      The standing-query explanation from section 5.3.

  S18 verification queue, empty
      "Nothing waiting for you." + a line about what will appear here.

  S16 my claims, empty
      "You have not claimed anything yet."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
16.2 Error Flows
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Validation failure
      Inline, field-level, on blur and again on submit. The message names the
      field and the correction: "Choose a date on or before today", not
      "Invalid input".

  409 on claim approval
      Handled in section 10.2. The message describes what happened in the
      world, not what happened in the database.

  Network failure mid-submission
      The form retains its contents. A banner offers retry. Form state is held
      in memory and, for the two report forms, mirrored to session storage, so
      that an accidental refresh does not discard ninety seconds of typing.

  403 on a route
      Redirect to S04 with a banner. No detail about what exists.

  404 on an item
      Generic not-found screen. A found item that has been claimed returns 404
      to a non-party, not 403, so that the claim status of an item is not
      disclosed by the error code.

  500
      A neutral error screen with a reference code that appears in the server
      log, so a student reporting a problem can quote something useful.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
16.3 Edge Cases
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The same user reports both sides. Prevented at match generation by BR-6's
IS DISTINCT FROM predicate. No match is created, and the user needs none.

Two claims arrive simultaneously. Section 10.2. One wins; the other receives a
specific, honest message.

A claimant withdraws after approval. Not permitted. The claim is APPROVED and
the path forward is either completion or staff reversal. Allowing withdrawal
after approval would leave the found item in a state nobody is responsible for.

The finder deletes their account while a claim is pending. Anonymisation, not
deletion: the claim survives, the verification routes to staff, and the audit
trail is intact. This is the concrete payoff of the anonymise-don't-delete
decision in the TRD.

A report is edited after a claim is filed against one of its matches. Blocked.
S12 offers Edit only while status is OPEN and no claim exists on any of its
matches, because editing the description after a claimant has committed to a
proof would undermine the verification.

An item is found before it is reported lost. Common and handled: the found
report is created first and sits AVAILABLE; when the lost report arrives,
generate_matches_for_lost finds it. The temporal component scores on the day
difference regardless of the order in which the reports were filed, which is why
the component keys on lost_date and found_date rather than on created_at. This
distinction is easy to get wrong and produces a subtly broken system when it is.

A user reports the same loss twice. Not blocked, because a person may genuinely
lose two similar items. The duplicate produces duplicate matches, which is
visible and harmless. Admin merge is a post-MVP item.

Clock skew across midnight. All timestamps are UTC at rest and converted at the
presentation layer, so an item reported at 00:30 IST is not displayed as the
previous day.

================================================================================
17. CROSS-CUTTING BEHAVIOURS
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
17.1 Loading
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Under 300 milliseconds: nothing. A spinner that flashes is worse than no
spinner.
300 milliseconds to two seconds: skeleton placeholders matching the shape of
the content that will arrive, so the layout does not jump.
Over two seconds: skeleton plus a progress message.
Form submission: the button enters a disabled loading state, which also prevents
the double-submit that would otherwise create two reports.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
17.2 Confirmation
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Destructive and irreversible actions require a modal confirmation: withdraw a
report, dismiss a match, approve a claim, reject a claim, dispose of an item.
Non-destructive actions do not, because a confirmation on every action trains
people to dismiss confirmations without reading them.

Dismissing a match is confirmed and additionally undoable for ten seconds. It is
the only action with both, because it is easy to trigger accidentally on a phone
and because dismissing the correct match is the single most damaging user error
available in the product.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
17.3 Feedback
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every state-changing action produces a visible acknowledgement within one second:
a toast, a banner, or a screen transition. An action that appears to do nothing
will be repeated, and a repeated claim submission is a real problem.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
17.4 Notifications (post-MVP)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A badge on the navigation bar, a notification centre at /notifications, and rows
generated on four events: a new STRONG match on one of your reports, a claim
filed on an item you found, a decision on a claim you filed, and a handover
confirmation. Polling every sixty seconds while a tab is focused is sufficient
and avoids a persistent connection.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
17.5 Responsive Behaviour
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The filter rail on S09 becomes a bottom sheet under 768 pixels. The two panels
on S14 stack, with the user's own report first. The verification screen S19 is
designed mobile-first, because it will overwhelmingly be used on a phone. Tables
in the staff console become card lists rather than horizontally scrolling tables,
because a horizontally scrolling table on a phone is unusable in practice.

18. END-TO-END SCENARIO WALKTHROUGHS
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
18.1 Scenario 1 — The Happy Path
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

10 September, 18:42. Ananya opens FoundIt on her phone. Not signed in, so S01.
She taps "I lost something", is routed to S03 with return=/report/lost,
registers instead, and lands on S06.

She types "AirPods". The category selector jumps to Electronics. She picks
Central Library from the location search after typing "lib". The date is already
10 September. She taps the white swatch, selects Apple, and types "black
charging case with a small scratch near the hinge". Elapsed: 71 seconds.

Submit. The transaction inserts the row, runs generate_matches_for_lost, finds
nothing above the floor, writes the audit row, commits in 94 milliseconds.

S08, third variant. "Your report is live. Nothing matching has been found yet.
From now on, every item anyone reports finding will be checked against your
description automatically." She browses, sees nothing, closes the app.

11 September, 09:15. Rahul finds the earphones on a library table. He reports
them on S07: "AirPods", Electronics, Central Library, today, white, Apple,
public description "white earphones in a black case", private detail "small blue
sticker inside the lid", custody "I left it at the Security Desk", storage
"Security Desk, Main Gate".

Submit. generate_matches_for_found runs. Ananya's lost item is OPEN, the
category matches, the date gap is one day, the brand matches, the colour matches,
the location matches, and the shared keywords are "black" and "case". Score: 20 + 20 + 20 +
15 + 13 + 10 = 98. Band: STRONG. The match row is inserted with its reason_json.

S08 for Rahul: "Thank you. We have recorded that it is at the Security Desk. We
have also found 1 person who reported losing something similar."

11 September, 10:58. Ananya opens the app. S04 shows a card: "1 strong match on
your AirPods report." She taps through to S14 and sees the two panels and the
breakdown summing to 98.

She taps "This is mine". S15 asks her to describe something distinctive. She
writes: "There's a small blue sticker inside the lid, and the case is scratched
near the hinge." Submit at 11:03.

11 September, 14:20. Rahul sees the verification prompt. S19 shows his own words
and hers side by side. The blue sticker is in both. He taps "Yes, it is theirs",
confirms, and approve_claim() runs: the found item locks, its status is
re-verified as AVAILABLE, the claim becomes APPROVED, the found item becomes
CLAIMED, the lost item becomes MATCHED, the match becomes CONFIRMED, and three
audit rows are written. All inside one transaction, 180 milliseconds.

Ananya's S17 now shows "Confirmed. This item is yours." with Rahul's contact
details and the collection instruction.

12 September, 10:07. She collects it from the desk and taps "I have collected
it". The completion transaction sets the claim to COMPLETED, the found item to
RETURNED, the lost item to RESOLVED, and writes three more audit rows. The
landing page counter increments.

S21 for her lost item now shows six timeline entries spanning two days.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
18.2 Scenario 2 — Contested Claim
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two students both report losing black earphones near the library on the same
day. Both are matched to one found item at scores of 87 and 84. Both file
claims within minutes of each other.

Rahul opens S18 and sees two pending claims on the same item. He opens the first
at 14:20:03 and the second at 14:20:05 in another tab.

He approves the first. The transaction locks found_items row 204, reads status
AVAILABLE, and proceeds through all nine steps including step 7, which rejects
the second claim with the note "Item claimed by another verified owner".

He then approves the second. Its transaction attempts the locking read, blocks
briefly, acquires the lock after the first commits, reads status CLAIMED, and
raises ITEM_NOT_AVAILABLE. The API returns 409. S19 shows: "Someone else's claim
on this item was approved a moment ago. Nothing has changed here."

The second claimant's S17 shows REJECTED with the neutral system note. Their
lost report returns to OPEN and continues matching against future finds. Their
flagged_count is not incremented, because the rejection was systemic rather than
a failed proof — a distinction the reject-by-verifier path and the
reject-by-transaction path must handle differently, and one that is easy to miss.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
18.3 Scenario 3 — Failed Proof
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A student browsing S09 sees a listed water bottle they would like. They have no
lost report, so S10 offers "This is mine" and routes them to S06 pre-filled with
the listing's category, location and colour.

They file the report. Because it was pre-filled from the listing, the match
scores 85 and appears immediately. They claim it, and S15 asks for a
distinguishing detail. They write "it's blue and it's a Milton" — both facts
taken from the public listing.

The finder sees their own private detail ("the name 'Sneha K' is written on the
base in marker") next to the claimant's answer. Nothing corresponds. They select
"The detail they gave does not match" and reject.

The claimant's S17 shows the neutral rejection. No indication of what the
correct answer was, and no indication of how close they came. Their
flagged_count increments to 1. Two more of these within thirty days and query
Q10 surfaces them on the staff dashboard.

The item returns to AVAILABLE and the match returns to SUGGESTED, remaining
available to a genuine owner.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
18.4 Scenario 4 — Found Before Lost
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A calculator is found in a lab on 5 September and reported the same day. No lost
report exists, so generate_matches_for_found produces nothing. The item sits
AVAILABLE.

On 8 September the owner realises it is missing and files a lost report dated 5
September. generate_matches_for_lost runs, finds the found item within the
thirty-day window, computes a date gap of zero days (found_date 5 September
minus lost_date 5 September), and awards the full 15 temporal points.

The match scores 90 and appears on the owner's confirmation screen immediately.
Three days elapsed between the loss and the report, and none of it mattered,
because the temporal component keys on the event dates rather than the report
dates. Had it keyed on created_at, the gap would have been three days and the
score would have been 81 — still strong, but wrong for the wrong reason, and
increasingly wrong as the reporting delay grows.
